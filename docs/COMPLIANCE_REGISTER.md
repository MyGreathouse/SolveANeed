# SolveANeed — UK Compliance Register (working draft)

> **This is not legal advice.** Every item below is an assumption that **requires verification by a qualified UK solicitor or compliance adviser**. SolveANeed has **no** regulatory approvals, registrations or authorisations at the time of writing, and must not claim any.

Status key: 🟥 blocking before launch · 🟧 required before the relevant feature · 🟨 ongoing obligation · ✅ built into the product (still needs legal review)

| # | Area | Assumed obligation (VERIFY) | Product controls in place | Open actions | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **UK GDPR & Data Protection Act 2018** | SolveANeed is the controller of member data. It needs a lawful basis for each purpose, a privacy notice, data-subject rights processes, records of processing, a DPIA (likely required: vulnerable users, financial-need data), processor contracts (Supabase, Vercel, email, payment provider) and international transfer safeguards. Must pay the ICO data protection fee. | Data minimisation (region only, no address; 18+ attestation instead of date of birth); private evidence; deletion and anonymisation flow; audit log; draft Privacy Notice | Complete DPIA and ROPA; pay ICO fee; sign DPAs; set retention periods; write the subject-access/export process; confirm Supabase region (EU/UK) | 🟥 |
| 2 | **PECR** (cookies, marketing email) | Consent is needed for non-essential cookies and for marketing emails. | Only strictly necessary auth cookies; marketing email off by default | Re-check before adding analytics | ✅ |
| 3 | **Online Safety Act 2023** | Members can post content (needs, and messages later), so SolveANeed is likely a regulated **user-to-user service**. Illegal-content duties have been in force since March 2025: a written illegal-content risk assessment, proportionate safety measures, reporting and complaints routes, and record keeping. A children's access assessment is also needed (accounts are 18+, but whether children are "likely to access" the service must still be assessed). | Pre-publication moderation; report button; moderator queue; suspension; audit trail; acceptable use policy; no public messaging in Phase 1 | Complete and record the illegal-content risk assessment and children's access assessment using Ofcom's templates; name a senior person accountable for online safety; publish a complaints procedure; review yearly | 🟥 |
| 4 | **Consumer protection** (Consumer Rights Act 2015, CPUT rules now in the DMCC Act 2024) | Clear information, no misleading practices, fees shown upfront (no drip pricing), no fake reviews or testimonials, fair contract terms. | No fake listings, stats or testimonials; "illustration" captions on stock images showing figures; fees to be shown before commitment; draft Terms | Legal review of Terms; fee disclosure design (Phase 4); review stock images showing figures | 🟧 |
| 5 | **Payment Services Regulations 2017 / EMRs 2011** | Holding or transferring other people's money is likely a regulated payment service. The FCA says online marketplaces can fall in scope. The commercial agent exclusion is narrow, and escrow-type arrangements typically don't qualify. The likely model is to use an authorised provider (e.g. Stripe Connect) so SolveANeed never holds client funds. | No payments built; live mode blocked in the database; no "escrow" claims in the product | Get a regulatory opinion on the payment flow (including PERG 15); confirm provider eligibility for each flow (gifts, task payments, milestones, supplier payments) and each currency (GBP/EUR/USD/NGN); document fund ownership | 🟥 (before Phase 4) |
| 6 | **Fundraising & charity law** | Personal crowdfunding isn't necessarily charitable fundraising, but Fundraising Regulator guidance and charity law may apply if charities take part or the platform describes giving as "charitable". Milestone sponsorship must not automatically be treated as charitable giving. Gift Aid is not available for gifts to individuals. | Copy avoids describing gifts as charitable donations or tax-deductible | Review all fundraising copy; decide policy on charity and CIC requesters; consider registering with the Fundraising Regulator if relevant | 🟧 |
| 7 | **Tax: digital platform reporting** | Since 1 January 2024, UK platform operators must collect and verify information on sellers of goods or services and report it to HMRC (and to the sellers) by **31 January** each year. Paid tasks (EARN) and possibly supplier payments are likely reportable. Operators must register with HMRC as reporting or excluded platform operators, and penalties apply. | Not yet applicable (no payments) | Confirm whether EARN, FUND and SOLVE count as "relevant activities"; plan seller due-diligence data collection (Phase 4); register with HMRC on time | 🟧 |
| 8 | **Employment status & worker protections** | Paid tasks must not create employment or worker status by accident (control, mutuality of obligation, personal service), and must avoid minimum-wage and IR35/off-payroll risk. The Employment Rights Act 2025 changes are being phased in. | Tasks are voluntary, opt-in, task-based; written deliverables; no ongoing obligation; early tasks limited to low-risk remote work | Legal opinion on the task model; task value and frequency limits; guidance for requesters on self-employment tax | 🟧 |
| 9 | **AML & sanctions** (MLRs 2017, OFSI) | Likely handled through the payment provider's KYC and sanctions screening. The platform still needs fraud and money-mule controls and must decide whether it is itself in scope. | Rate limits; duplicate-need and duplicate-description risk signals; moderation; account suspension | Confirm the provider's KYC coverage; set transaction limits; write a suspicious activity escalation procedure | 🟧 |
| 10 | **Accessibility** (Equality Act 2010; target WCAG 2.2 AA) | Reasonable adjustments; accessible service. | axe-core WCAG 2.2 A/AA checks on 8 key pages (desktop and mobile) pass; keyboard-accessible navigation; colour never the only signal | Manual screen-reader audit; accessibility statement page | ✅ / 🟨 |
| 11 | **Complaints handling** | Accessible complaints route; Online Safety Act complaints duties; ADR information where applicable. | Report flow; moderation notes to requesters | Publish complaints and appeals policy with timescales; build appeal UI (Phase 3) | 🟧 |
| 12 | **Dispute resolution** | Fair, documented process for task and milestone disputes; provider chargeback rules. | Schema for disputes and evidence (Phase 3) | Write the dispute policy; align it with the provider's dispute and chargeback process | 🟧 |
| 13 | **Age** | 18+ only. Self-attestation may not be enough if risk assessments call for stronger age assurance. | Database rejects sign-ups without the 18+ attestation | Decide whether age assurance or ID checks are needed (Online Safety Act / risk-based) | 🟨 |
| 14 | **Company & trading disclosures** | The operating entity's name, registered office and number must appear on the website and in emails. | Placeholder text in Terms and Privacy Notice | Insert entity details before launch | 🟥 |
